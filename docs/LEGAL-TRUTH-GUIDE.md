# SherpaCarta — Legal and Truth Guide

This guide is mandatory for public copy, formal documents, campaign pages, press responses, and AI-assisted edits.

## What SherpaCarta is

SherpaCarta is a public-domain living charter and civic reference document about digital rights. It is published by a Give A Bit family project and may be read, discussed, adapted, translated, and used as model language subject to the stated license.

## What it is not

SherpaCarta is not, by itself:

- legislation or a treaty;
- a court judgment or legal opinion;
- an official government petition;
- proof that a signer’s identity was verified;
- proof that a claim in the document is legally enforceable;
- an endorsement by any organization unless that organization is explicitly and verifiably identified;
- a guarantee that a Bitcoin stamp is confirmed.

## Required claim vocabulary

Use **public mandate** for the SherpaCarta campaign. Use **official e-petition** only for a live government-hosted petition with its official identifier. Use **paper petition** only for the relevant original paper process.

Use **local-first** when data is stored in the browser by default. Do not say “we collect no data” if an optional action sends a receipt hash or limited metadata to an API; describe the exact fields and purpose.

Use **Bitcoin-stamped** only when the content was submitted to a Bitcoin timestamp/proof workflow. Use **Bitcoin-confirmed** only when the proof service reports confirmation. Pending is never confirmed.

## Canada language

The Canada offering has separate tracks:

1. public digital mandate;
2. federal paper petition;
3. official House of Commons e-petition if and when an MP sponsors it and an official identifier is published.

Never merge their totals or imply that a campaign signature automatically counts in Parliament.

## Proof language

A SHA-256 hash is a fingerprint of a particular byte/text representation. A timestamp proof can provide evidence that the hash existed by a given time according to the proof system. It does not prove the truth of the underlying text, its authorship, its legal effect, or the identity of every participant.

## Formal-document rule

Adding a logo, letterhead, signature block, seal, PDF formatting, or executive title does not itself create legal force. Formal documents must retain their version, date, source URL, legal qualification, and verification instructions.

## Data and privacy rule

Do not request identity documents, private keys, passwords, payment secrets, or unnecessary contact data. Do not expose signer names, receipt contents, or local browser data through logs, metrics, analytics, URLs, screenshots, or support emails.

## Review checklist

Before publishing a claim, ask:

- Is this directly supported by a current source file or authoritative external record?
- Is the status current: planned, live, pending, confirmed, or official?
- Could a reasonable reader mistake this for law, government action, or identity verification?
- Does the text reveal or request unnecessary personal or secret information?
- Can the reader independently inspect the source, version, hash, or status?

When uncertain, use narrower language and link to the evidence.
