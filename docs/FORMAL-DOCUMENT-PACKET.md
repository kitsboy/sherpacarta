SherpaCarta is intended to be:

## One-page executive brief

### The issue
Digital systems increasingly determine what people see, access, pay, qualify for, and are presumed to be. Existing protections are fragmented across jurisdictions and often do not clearly address private platforms, data brokers, automated decisions, or surveillance at scale.

### The proposal
SherpaCarta publishes 114 articles as a readable, living reference charter for digital human rights. It extends the constitutional tradition into networked life while recognizing that law, policy, and enforcement remain jurisdiction-specific.

### Core principles
1. Privacy is a human right and should be the default.
2. People should control data generated by or about them.
3. Digital expression requires transparent, appealable governance.
4. Internet access and strong encryption support human participation.
5. High-impact automated decisions require explanation and human review.
6. Children, vulnerable people, and communities require heightened protection.
7. Amendments may expand rights but must not silently reduce them.
8. Public claims must remain distinguishable from legal or official government outcomes.

### Participation
Readers can use the public site to read the charter, make a local-first civic commitment, participate in optional public discussion, and inspect document/hash proof. National campaigns may have separate paper or official government routes; those routes must never be represented as the same thing as a SherpaCarta campaign signature.

### Evidence and integrity
The project publishes document versions and can use Satohash to anchor a SHA-256 hash. The hash identifies a specific content representation. A pending submission is not Bitcoin-confirmed. Independent verification is encouraged.

### Governance and funding
SherpaCarta is open source and released under CC0 1.0 Universal. Funding rails and campaign statistics are disclosed separately from document claims. No endorsement, official status, signature total, or legal effect should be inferred unless specifically documented and independently verifiable.

## Plain-language summary

SherpaCarta is a document about rights in the digital world. It says people should not have to give up privacy, control of their data, fair treatment, or freedom of expression just to use modern technology.

You can read it without signing in. You can make a voluntary local commitment without creating an account. You can choose whether to participate in optional public discussion. If a version is stamped through Satohash, you can check the hash and status yourself.

The project is not claiming that the charter is already law. It is offering language that people can discuss, improve, cite, and take into appropriate legal or democratic processes.

## Technical disclosure

### Source and build
- Charter source: `data/charter.json`.
- Build pipeline generates public API, article files, plain-text export, sitemap, and client-side charter data.
- Public API: `/api/v1/`.
- Release metadata: `/release-manifest.json`.
- Public hash endpoint: `/api/v1/hash.json`.

### Client privacy
- Reading does not require an account.
- Core signing is local-first and uses browser storage.
- Optional public features may use Nostr or campaign APIs and are labeled separately.
- Private keys, nsecs, invoice keys, organizer tokens, and credentials must not be placed in source control.

### Proof model
1. A document representation is hashed with SHA-256.
2. The hash can be submitted to Satohash using the SherpaCarta family reference.
3. The proof has a lifecycle, including pending and confirmed states.
4. The public UI must never call pending evidence Bitcoin-confirmed.
5. A verifier compares the exact content, hash, proof ID, status, and canonical URLs.

### Security boundaries
- The public site is not an identity provider.
- A local signature is not identity verification.
- Campaign totals are not automatically official government totals.
- Nostr publication is optional and uses user-controlled signing extensions.
- Administrative operations require separate secrets and controls.

## Legal and policy qualification

This packet is a communications and technical reference, not legal advice. It does not create a contract, fiduciary relationship, agency relationship, employment relationship, government petition, or representation that a public body has adopted SherpaCarta.

Before using SherpaCarta in a legal filing, legislative submission, public campaign, or institutional policy, obtain review from qualified counsel and the relevant authority. Jurisdiction-specific requirements control. The Canada pathway, for example, separates the public mandate, federal paper process, and any official House of Commons e-petition.

The words **live**, **published**, **stamped**, **pending**, and **confirmed** must be used precisely:

- **Published:** available from the stated public source.
- **Stamped:** a hash was submitted or recorded by the stated proof service.
- **Pending:** proof processing or Bitcoin confirmation is incomplete.
- **Bitcoin-confirmed:** the proof service reports the relevant Bitcoin confirmation state.
- **Official:** adopted, issued, or hosted by the named public authority—not merely linked by SherpaCarta.
