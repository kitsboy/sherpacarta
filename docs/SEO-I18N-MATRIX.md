# Multilingual SEO and rights-language matrix

**Status:** planning and implementation specification. Translation approval is not claimed.

## Canonical audience framing

SherpaCarta is a secular, nonpartisan, public-domain charter about rights in digital life. “Magna Carta” is a historical analogy about limits on power—not a religious claim, political party, or assertion of current law.

## Metadata rules

1. Every localized title and description must be reviewed by a fluent human.
2. Never translate “official,” “binding,” “confirmed,” or “government adopted” more strongly than the source evidence supports.
3. Use equivalent-page `hreflang` only when the page actually exists and content is meaningfully equivalent.
4. Preserve article numbers and source-release identifiers.
5. Add language-specific social descriptions and alt text.
6. Mark partial/community translations visibly.
7. Keep religious-neutral language: rights, dignity, public discussion, history, law, and technology.
8. Avoid inflammatory words implying enemies, conquest, salvation, or inevitable conflict.

## Recommended page families

- `/` — global charter overview
- `/share.html` — sharing and context
- `/verify.html` — proof and verification
- `/archive.html` — releases and provenance
- `/canada/` — Canada-specific civic pathway
- `/jurisdictions.html` — jurisdiction boundaries
- `/press-kit.html` — media/institutional resources

## SEO evidence fields

For each localized page record: locale, URL, title, description, canonical, `hreflang` group, source release/hash, reviewer status, last review date, unresolved terms, and demo/pending disclosures.

## No-ranking promises

Do not claim search rankings, global reach, language adoption, or user counts without current evidence. SEO optimization improves discoverability; it does not prove public support or legal legitimacy.
