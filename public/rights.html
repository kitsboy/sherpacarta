Digital rights, plainly

The rights we need
in digital life.

Digital rights are principles for protecting human dignity when technology affects our privacy, choices, work, identity, relationships, and access to public life. This is a secular, nonpartisan educational page—not a religious statement and not current law.

01

Privacy

You should be able to communicate and live without unnecessary surveillance, profiling, or exposure.

02

Data control

You should understand what data is held about you and have meaningful ways to access, correct, delete, or move it.

03

Expression

Digital participation should include clear rules, transparent moderation, and a meaningful route to appeal.

04

Access

Essential digital services should be accessible and should not exclude people unfairly.

05

Explanation

When an automated system makes a consequential decision, people deserve understandable reasons and human review.

06

Collective dignity

Children, vulnerable people, communities, and Indigenous data interests deserve careful protection.

Why “Magna Carta”?

We use the phrase as a historical analogy for limiting concentrated power. It is not a religious appeal, a claim of legal continuity, or a declaration that this charter is already enforceable.
